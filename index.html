The story of the dhaba

Long before food apps and expressways, India's highways ran on dhabas — roadside stops where truck drivers, families, and night travellers pulled over for hot chai, tandoori roti, and paratha fresh off the tawa. The word dhaba comes from Punjabi roadside culture, spreading along Grand Trunk routes and national highways from the 1940s onward.

By the 1960s and 70s, dhabas became small kingdoms of warmth: charpai seats under string lights, a crackling tandoor, Coca-Cola posters on tin walls, and a radio humming filmi songs into the dark. NH-1, NH-44, and every dusty bypass had its own ढाबा वाला — the person who kept the kettle boiling till sunrise.

In the 90s, tape decks replaced transistor radios. Truck cabins played cassettes; dhaba counters kept the same hits on loop — Kumar Sanu, Alka Yagnik, the golden era of Bollywood romance drifting across foggy highways. That sound is what this station remembers.

About this site

What is Dhaba Wala Radio?

Dhaba Wala Radio is a static roadside music station built by @guru.shetty.x. It recreates the feeling of stopping at a night highway dhaba — neon sign glowing, chai steaming, playlist rolling — without leaving your browser.

The player runs on a curated 90s Bollywood playlist and shifts mood with India Standard Time: morning tandoor hours, afternoon chai counter, evening thali, and late-night highway raat. Hit play and let the signal ride.
